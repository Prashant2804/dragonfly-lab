# Walking the site as the buyer

**Rajesh Menon, 41.** Partner in a 12-person survey firm in Coimbatore. Ran Trimble kit at a
previous employer; today the firm owns two CHC units bought through a Chennai dealer. He found
the site on his phone, mid-site-visit, searching *"rtk gnss receiver price india"*, and reopened
it that evening on a 1366×768 office laptop. He is considering two rovers and one base. He has
never heard of Dragonfly.

What follows is what he hits, in the order he hits it. Twenty issues. Fourteen are fixed;
six need a decision from you.

---

## Blocking — trust

**1. There is no phone number anywhere. ⚠️ NEEDS YOU**
For Indian B2B capital equipment this is the first thing checked and the fastest way to be
dismissed. `site.phone` exists in the config and is empty. Give me a number and it goes in the
header, the footer, the contact page and the `LocalBusiness` schema.

**2. The contact address is a gmail.com address. ⚠️ NEEDS YOU**
Selling a lakh-plus instrument from a personal Gmail undercuts every other credibility signal on
the site. `hello@dragonflylab.in` costs nothing and changes how the whole thing reads.

**3. Nothing answered "what happens when it breaks". ✅ FIXED**
This is the biggest objection for someone leaving a dealer relationship, and the site was silent
on warranty, lead time, service turnaround, training and what is in the box. There is now a
**Buying & support** section on the product page and all four solution pages, plus six FAQs
covering breakage, warranty, delivery, training, workflow migration and seeing one first.
⚠️ **The values are conservative placeholders in `src/data/commerce.mjs` and are commitments to
customers — confirm every one before launch.**

**4. No people. ⚠️ NEEDS YOU**
No founder, no team, no faces, no office. Buying an unknown instrument is a bet on the people
behind it, and there are none visible. A founder photo and three lines about you would do more
than any feature card on the site.

**5. Every social share rendered a broken image. ✅ FIXED — this was a real bug**
Every page pointed `og:image` at `/img/og-default.png`, which did not exist. Every WhatsApp and
LinkedIn share showed a broken card — in a market where survey WhatsApp groups *are* the
distribution channel. There is now a proper 1200×630 card, generated by
`node scripts/render-og.mjs`.

## Comprehension

**6. The hero image was unreadable. ✅ FIXED**
It was a crop of the middle of the pole: an anonymous grey cylinder with fins. The single most
important image on the site and he could not tell what the product was. The homepage now leads
with the **base station** — a tripod is instantly recognisable as survey equipment — and the
solution pages each show the configuration that buyer would actually order.

**7. No sense of scale. ✅ FIXED**
Nothing told him whether this was a 30 cm gadget or a 2 m pole. Hero images now carry a
dimension line (`1.44 m` on the base, `2.00 m` on the rover) drawn in the page rather than baked
into the render, so it stays crisp and editable.

**8. The mobile first screen was 100% text. ✅ FIXED**
Seven lines of paragraph before any picture, on a phone, during a site visit. The image now
comes first on mobile, the sub-heading drops a size, and the two buttons are equal width instead
of ragged.

**9. Four solution pages that look identical. ✅ FIXED**
Same layout, same hero image, no way to tell at a glance which one he was on. Each now has its
own hero subject — drone, rover, base, base — and the drone page finally shows the drone.

**10. Everything is a render; nothing is a photograph. ⚠️ NEEDS YOU**
Renders read as "not built yet". One phone photo of the rover standing in a field would convert
harder than anything else on the site.

## Defects found

**11. The comparison table header was clipped. ✅ FIXED — real bug**
The sticky `<thead>` was pinned to `top: 0`, which is *under* the 62px sticky site header, so the
column titles were sliced in half — on the page a comparison shopper came to read. It is now
offset by a `--hdr-h` variable, layered above the body rows, and given an opaque background so
the first data row no longer bleeds through it.

**12. Small text failed WCAG AA. ✅ FIXED**
`--muted` was `#737E87` — 4.30:1 on the card background, under the 4.5:1 minimum, and it is used
for form notes, captions and the footer legal text. Now `#8B949D` (5.2:1).

**13. No print stylesheet. ✅ FIXED**
He will print or PDF the comparison and spec pages into a procurement file. A dark site prints as
a black rectangle. There is now a print stylesheet that inverts to paper, drops the navigation,
forms and 3D, expands the FAQ answers, prints link destinations after the link text, and keeps
tables from breaking across pages.

## The 3D models you sent

**19. The rover and base station STEP exports are missing their main tubes.**
Both files come from a three.js faceted writer, and both are missing exactly the part that holds
everything together: the rover has **no pole and no tip** — just five collars, a phone cradle and
the head assembly floating in space — and the base station has **no mast** between the tripod head
and the receiver stack.

They also carry no part names, no colours and no assembly tree: 122–157 anonymous solids each.

So the converter now rebuilds the structure itself. It groups solids into named modules by
position along the model axis (`antenna`, `gateway`, `battery_module`, `pole_collars`,
`tripod_legs`, `drone_airframe`…) so hotspots and the exploded view still work, and it
**synthesises the missing tubes** from the dimensions in your own parametric FreeCAD script.

⚠️ **That synthesised geometry is mine, not yours.** It is visually right and dimensionally
plausible; it is not authoritative. Delete the `"tubes"` block from `cad/plans/*.json` the moment
you send a corrected export. My guess at the cause is that the pole was transparent or hidden in
the source when it was exported.

**20. The drone model is complete and excellent. ✅ USED**
Quadcopter airframe, arms, props, landing gear, and the Aerom module mounted on top. It is now
the hero of the drone-mapping page, a card on the homepage, and a third model in the interactive
viewer. That page finally shows the thing it is selling.

Detail in the head and antenna is much better than the old FreeCAD-derived models across all
three, so the upgrade was worth doing even with the missing tubes.
